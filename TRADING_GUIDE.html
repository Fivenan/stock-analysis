TRADING GUIDE

# Trading guide: understanding, testing and paper practice

This course uses EUR accounts and invented numbers. It teaches questions and reproducible experiments, not an expected return or a recommendation. Use market access, providers, broker research, tax/regulation and the dated source register for externally sourced claims. Documentation covers more markets and instruments than the executable sample supports. Current fees, eligibility, taxation and entitlements must be checked against the linked official source before any real decision.

## Beginner: ownership, venues and prices

A share is a residual ownership claim; a bond is a contractual debt claim with maturity, default and rate risk. A fund pools assets; an ETF trades on a venue and may differ from its net asset value. Index exposure is a rule-based basket, not necessarily a product available to you. Currency is both a quotation unit and a risk factor. Derivatives reference an underlying and can embed leverage, expiry, margin and nonlinear losses. The local app does not model them.

A stock exchange matches orders under venue rules. The highest bid is what a buyer currently offers; the lowest ask is what a seller requests. A EUR100 bid and EUR100.20 ask imply a EUR0.20 spread, about 20 basis points of midpoint EUR100.10. A round trip crossing both sides loses roughly EUR0.20 per share before fees if nothing moves. Displayed depth is quantity at successive prices; it can disappear. Volume is completed trades, not a promise you can transact that volume at one price. Thin overseas hours can have wider spreads than overlapping home-market hours. A broker's reference-market chart is not a fill guarantee.

A market order prioritizes execution, not price. A limit order constrains price but may never fill. A stop triggers an order after a threshold and can gap beyond it. A stop-limit can trigger yet remain unfilled. The app simulates daily next-open market orders only; its spread/slippage assumptions are not a real order book. Session calendars, time zones and daylight-saving changes govern which information is actually available. Settlement happens after execution under the instrument/venue rules; a backtest that immediately reuses proceeds makes a simplifying cash assumption. Confirm actual broker settlement and trading restrictions separately.

Price changes reflect both cash-flow expectations and required returns. A company can grow profits while its share falls if expectations were higher or discount rates rise. Earnings surprises, guidance, capital structure, competition, liquidity and regulation interact. A thesis such as “semiconductor demand will grow” lacks entry, exit, valuation, instrument, timing and falsification rules. A strategy must specify all of these before testing and can still fail despite a correct economic story.

## Beginner: returns, corporate actions and EUR translation

Simple return is `r = (ending value + distributions − starting value) / starting value`. Log return is `ln(1+r)`; log returns add over time while simple returns compound. +10% then −10% gives `1.1 × 0.9 − 1 = −1%`, not zero. Log returns are undefined at −100% and must not conceal bankruptcy.

A EUR account holding USD assets has both local price and exchange-rate exposure. Define `f` as EUR per one USD, never the inverse. EUR return before costs is `(P1/P0) × (f1/f0) − 1`. USD100 rises to USD110 while f falls from 0.90 to 0.81: EUR90 becomes EUR89.10, so return is −1%, despite +10% local price return. A security trading in EUR may still own foreign businesses; quotation currency and underlying economic exposure differ.

Dividend example: one share marked EUR100 becomes EUR99 at ex date with EUR1 entitlement. Equity remains EUR100 if the dividend receivable is recognized. Payment changes receivable to cash, not another EUR1 gain. A sale between ex and payment does not erase the recorded entitlement. Tax/withholding and conversion fees can alter real outcomes; this engine models zero dividend FX fee and no tax withholding. Adjusted total-return prices plus separately credited dividends double-count returns.

Split example: one share at EUR100 becomes two at EUR50 in a 2-for-1 split. Quantity doubles, per-share basis halves, total basis and equity remain EUR100. Raw close returns show a mechanical −50%; action-safe research returns do not. The app exposes raw prices and a separate action-safe index. Delisting is different: unavailable quotes do not establish zero loss or a recoverable terminal value. Unknown proceeds must stop a trustworthy valuation.

Cash accounting example: EUR1,000 buys 10 shares at USD100 with f=0.90. Consideration is EUR900 and cash becomes EUR100 before costs. A EUR1 fixed fee plus EUR0.45 FX fee makes cash EUR98.55. If the next mark remains USD100 but f becomes 1.00, assets are EUR1,000 and total equity EUR1,098.55. FX gain is not a local stock gain. Buys consume consideration and costs; sells receive proceeds less their own costs. FIFO realized P&L allocates acquisition and disposal costs to matched lots. Open lots have unrealized gains, not completed winning trades.

## Intermediate: analyzing a business and an economy

Read income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement together. Revenue growth with worsening receivables can differ from cash generation. Operating income excludes financing; net income includes it. Free cash flow is often approximated as operating cash flow minus capital expenditure, but definitions and accounting adjustments must be documented. Net debt, interest coverage, dilution, pension obligations and off-balance commitments affect ownership value. A low P/E can reflect cyclical peak earnings or structural decline rather than cheapness.

A discounted cash-flow estimate is `V = Σ CF_t/(1+k)^t + terminal value/(1+k)^T`. EUR10 each year for two years at 10% has present value `10/1.1 + 10/1.1² = EUR17.36`. Terminal assumptions often dominate a long-horizon estimate; show a grid of growth/discount-rate cases instead of a precise target. Enterprise value and equity value differ by financing claims. Compare multiples only across compatible accounting, leverage, business models and cycles. Fundamental data must carry publication dates; fiscal period end is not the day investors knew the number.

Technical analysis summarizes price/volume behavior. A trailing SMA is the mean of the last L completed closes. Trend rules hypothesize persistence; momentum ranks past returns; mean reversion hypothesizes reversal after deviation. Indicators are transforms of existing observations, not independent evidence. Adding five correlated indicators rarely supplies five independent reasons. A moving average crossing after the close cannot execute at that same close in this engine. Split normalization must use only actions effective and available by the decision time.

Macroeconomic relationships are conditional. Central-bank tightening can raise discount rates, slow lending and support a currency, yet an already-anticipated hike can produce the opposite immediate price response. Nominal rates approximately combine real rates and expected inflation; bond prices generally fall as required yields rise, with duration approximating local sensitivity `ΔP/P ≈ −D × Δyield`. A duration-6 instrument facing a one percentage point yield increase has approximately −6% price sensitivity before convexity. This is educational, not an implemented bond model.

Business cycles affect sectors through demand, inventories, financing and pricing power. Rotation and factor exposures (value, size, quality, momentum) can remain out of favor for years. Cross-market correlation is estimated, unstable and often rises during stress. Commodity price analysis needs supply, demand, inventories, capacity, substitution and transport constraints. Physical metal, futures, an ETC/ETN, a mining share and a mining ETF have different storage, roll, issuer, operational and equity risks. Nickel exposure through an Indonesian exporter adds country, currency and company risk; it does not replicate nickel spot returns. China home listings, Hong Kong listings and depositary receipts have different access and legal structures. Read the access matrix rather than assuming a data symbol is tradable.

Geopolitical risk can interrupt custody, settlement, ownership rights or capital conversion. A broad diversified label does not remove common suppliers, energy prices or policy exposure. An earnings forecast and a macro forecast should be expressed as ranges with observable rejection conditions: e.g. lower volumes, inventory build, financing deterioration or a policy change. Avoid substituting a compelling narrative for measured data.

## Intermediate: statistics and portfolio construction

For daily returns `r_i`, arithmetic mean is `Σr_i/n`; sample variance is `Σ(r_i−mean)²/(n−1)`. Annualized sample volatility uses `std × sqrt(252)` here, a disclosed convention rather than a universal count of trading days. Geometric annual return uses elapsed calendar time: `(E_end/E_start)^(365.25/days)−1`. Arithmetic mean ×252 is different and can overstate compound growth. Standard deviation treats gains/losses symmetrically and is incomplete for tail risk.

Covariance measures joint deviations; correlation divides covariance by the product of standard deviations. Portfolio variance is `wᵀΣw`. Two 50% positions each with 20% volatility and zero correlation have volatility `sqrt(.25×.04 + .25×.04) = 14.14%`; if correlation is one, it is 20%. These inputs are estimates, not guarantees. Covariance matrices estimated from a short, changing history can make optimized weights unstable. Constraints and simple baselines often expose more useful research questions than unconstrained optimization.

Position sizing must account for equity, concentration, liquidity and plausible losses. If a hypothetical EUR10,000 policy allows EUR100 loss on a EUR5 per-share stress, naive size is 20 shares before costs. A stop does not guarantee the EUR5 maximum, and a gap can exceed it. Apply a separate notional cap and liquidity cap, then test the combined worst case. The app enforces long-only position and order/volume limits; it does not execute stop-based risk sizing. Choose your own policy values rather than copying this example.

Drawdown is `equity/running peak − 1`. EUR100→80 is −20%; recovery requires +25%, not +20%. Duration measures time underwater and may remain unrecovered at the endpoint. Sharpe here is mean daily excess / sample daily excess std ×sqrt(252). Sortino uses the root mean squared negative excess returns across all observations. Calmar is CAGR / absolute maximum drawdown. Flat equity can make denominators undefined: “None” with an explanation is more truthful than an infinite score. Winning-trade percentage excludes open FIFO lots and does not imply profit if losing trades are much larger. Two-sided turnover here is absolute traded EUR / (2 × mean equity). Inspect the methodology before comparing metrics from another platform.

## Advanced: designing a falsifiable experiment

Write a hypothesis before choosing parameters: “positive trailing local total-return momentum among the specified universe may persist enough after EUR costs to beat a same-cost equal-weight benchmark.” Freeze universe eligibility, lookback, ranking tie-break, top N, observation timing, monthly schedule, execution lag, weights, fees and reject conditions. Record an experiment ID and source hashes. A synthetic run verifies plumbing; it cannot test that economic hypothesis.

Survivorship bias occurs when today's surviving instruments replace the historically available universe. Look-ahead bias occurs when a future value or publication affects an earlier decision. Revised macro data needs vintages: today's revised GDP is not what a historical trader knew. Selection bias arises from choosing markets or start dates after seeing results. Delisted assets, missing sessions, FX direction errors, dividend double counting and split-induced signals can dominate the supposed edge. Do not fill missing execution prices with future values. Old marks can be flagged valuations within limits, not executable prices.

Train/validation/test periods must be chronological, with leakage-aware separation where overlapping labels are used. Use training to define a candidate, validation to choose among planned alternatives and an untouched test once for evaluation. Repeatedly consulting the test set converts it into training. Walk-forward refits only on past data then evaluates later segments; expanding windows and rolling windows encode different assumptions about regime persistence. These are planned research features, not implemented app buttons. A written manual research protocol is not evidence that automated OOS validation occurred.

Multiple testing: if 100 independent ineffective rules each have a 5% false-positive chance, the chance of at least one apparent success is `1−0.95^100 ≈ 99.4%`. Tests are often correlated, but the selection problem remains. Register the number of tried rules, include failures and consider appropriate corrections before a claim. A p-value measures data incompatibility with a specified null under assumptions, not the probability a strategy is profitable. Economic magnitude, costs, dependence, confidence intervals and replication matter.

Regression `return = α + β×market + error` can distinguish broad market exposure from residual returns only under its sample/model assumptions. Autocorrelation invalidates naive independent-observation standard errors. Nonstationarity means distributions or relationships change over time. Apparent regimes defined after the outcome are another form of leakage. Fat tails, skew and volatility clustering make a normal-return model an incomplete stress model. Resample-based or Monte Carlo analysis must declare its dependence assumptions; shuffling daily returns can destroy important path structure. This app does not implement such methods or claim calibrated probabilities.

Machine learning adds model capacity and opportunities for leakage. Fit scalers/imputers on training only; align features by their real publication time; purge overlapping labels where needed; compare against simple baselines after the same costs. Feature importance is not causal proof. Reject a model that needs future revisions, cannot survive modest cost changes, relies on a single year/listing, or cannot be reproduced from immutable inputs. A sensitivity surface with a broad stable region is more informative than one best point, but remains in-sample if evaluated on reused data.

## Operational practice and configurable policy

An investment policy statement should be filled in by the user: purpose ___; horizon ___; cash needs ___; capital genuinely available for risk ___; maximum tolerable loss in EUR and % ___; permitted instruments/venues ___; single-position and country/currency caps ___; liquidity reserve ___; review schedule ___; prohibited behavior ___; escalation/recovery process ___. Unknown fields remain unknown. No tool can infer your tolerance from an illustrative backtest.

A research risk plan records data-source license, timestamp, raw/adjusted basis, FX convention, calendar assumption, capital/costs, order limits, stale-data threshold, experiment registry and stop-research conditions. A paper order checklist asks whether the hypothesis, entry/exit plan, quantity and next-open timestamp match the plan; after filling, reconcile cash/holdings/fees and note differences. Keep journals append-only. Distinguish an intentional new order from a replay. Back up private runtime data with the app stopped. Never publish account records with static educational material.

## Exercises and worked solutions

1. EUR translation: a JPY share rises 8%, while EUR per JPY falls 5%. Solution: `1.08×.95−1 = 2.6%` before costs, not 3% exactly.
2. Split/dividend: two shares at EUR60 split 3-for-1, then pay EUR0.50 per post-split share. Raw price after ex is EUR19.50. Solution: six shares worth EUR117 plus EUR3 entitlement = EUR120. Cash payment transfers EUR3 from receivable to cash. Total basis remains EUR120 before any other changes.
3. Costs: buy five units at EUR40, fixed fee EUR1, commission 10 bps. Solution: consideration EUR200, commission EUR0.20, total cash debit EUR201.20 before spread/slippage. Do not subtract those fees again from end equity.
4. Compounding: +20%, −20%, +5% from EUR1,000. Solution: `1000×1.2×.8×1.05 = EUR1008`; +0.8% total. Average simple return is 1.667% per period and is not the compound result.
5. Concentration: EUR3,000 in one listing, EUR2,000 in another and EUR5,000 cash. Solution: total equity EUR10,000, position weights 30%/20%, invested exposure 50%. Country weights may sum to 50% when cash is separately shown. Cash belongs in the denominator.
6. Drawdown: EUR100→120→90→110. Solution: maximum drawdown `90/120−1 = −25%`, still unrecovered at EUR110. A 33.33% gain from EUR90 is needed to regain EUR120.
7. Leakage: use an earnings report released March 10 to trade March 1 because its period ended December 31. Solution: invalid; align availability to March 10 (or later actual public availability), then use the next allowable execution event.
8. Order replay: submit PAPER-X, restart and submit the same payload. Solution: one durable fill, returned again. Change quantity under PAPER-X: reject canonical conflict. New intention requires a new ID and nondecreasing simulation time.
9. Hypothesis audit: a momentum rule wins only with zero fees in one selected country/year. Solution: reject a robustness/profitability claim; document selection, rerun planned costs and untouched periods with lawful real data. The synthetic app cannot repair the evidentiary gap.
10. Policy exercise: fill every IPS blank and invent two stress scenarios, including one correlated price/FX loss. Solution rubric: values must be user supplied, internally consistent and within cash/liquidity needs; passing is a documented review, not adopting any numeric template here.

## Glossary

Alpha: residual return relative to a specified model. Ask/bid: lowest sell/highest buy quote. Basis point: 0.01 percentage point. Benchmark: declared comparison portfolio. CAGR: compounded annual growth by elapsed time. Corporate action: event changing ownership/cash entitlement. Covariance: joint deviations from means. Drawdown: decline from prior equity peak. Ex date: entitlement boundary. Exposure: invested value relative to equity. FIFO: first acquired lot matched first. FX: currency conversion. Liquidity: ability to trade size at acceptable cost. MIC: venue identifier. Momentum: persistence hypothesis based on past return. NAV: fund net asset value. Overfitting: fitting sample noise that fails later. Point in time: only information available by the decision. Provenance: source, retrieval and immutable identity. Receivable: owned payment claim not yet cash. Rebalance: restore target weights. Regime: hypothesized state of market dynamics. Slippage: execution difference beyond declared reference. Spread: ask minus bid. Survivorship: omission of failed/delisted instruments. TWR: chain-linked return excluding external-flow effects. UCITS/PRIIPs/KID: regulatory/product concepts requiring instrument-specific official checks. Volatility: dispersion of returns under a stated convention.
