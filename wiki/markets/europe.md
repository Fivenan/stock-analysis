## 1. Overview

Europe has no single stock exchange. It has several national venues, some grouped into operators, all quoting in different currencies and under different tax rules.

- **Euronext** is the largest pan-European group. Its regulated markets are Amsterdam, Athens, Brussels, Dublin, Lisbon, Milan, Oslo and Paris [2]. As of June 2026 they host over 1,800 listed issuers with EUR 7 trillion of market capitalisation [1]. (The same release's footer says "As of September 2026" for the same figures, so the date is ambiguous; this page uses June 2026.)
- **Nasdaq Nordic and Baltic** (Stockholm, Copenhagen, Helsinki, Iceland and the Baltics) had a combined market value of EUR 2,061 billion in June 2026 [3]. The figure includes the Baltics, so it is not Nordic-only.
- **London Stock Exchange (LSE)**, **SIX Swiss Exchange** and **BME** (Bolsas y Mercados Españoles, Madrid) are separate venues. SIX and BME both belong to the SIX group [4]. Total market cap and listing counts for the LSE, SIX and BME could not be verified from an extractable official source (the WFE statistics pages were not machine-readable); see Open questions.
- Size indicators from official releases: SIX Swiss Exchange equity turnover was CHF 874.8 billion in 2025; BME equity turnover was EUR 384.6 billion [4].

## 3. Trading hours

German time is CET in winter and CEST in summer. The table shows times in CET/CEST.

| Exchange | Local time | German time (CET/CEST) | Phases and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Euronext (Paris, Amsterdam, Brussels, Lisbon, Milan, Dublin) | Same as CET/CEST (Dublin 08:00-16:30 local) | 09:00-17:30 | Opening uncrossing at 09:00 (random within 30 s), closing uncrossing 17:35 (random), trading-at-last to 17:40 [9]; Dublin local times are one hour behind CET [10] |
| Euronext Oslo | Same as CET/CEST | 09:00-16:20 (unverified) | Closing auction about 16:25 (unverified) |
| LSE | 08:00-16:30 UK | 09:00-17:30 | Opening auction 07:50-08:00, closing auction 16:30-16:35 (unverified; the LSE page was not machine-readable) |
| SIX Swiss Exchange | Same as CET/CEST | 09:00-17:30 | Opening auction 09:00 (random within two minutes), closing auction 17:20-17:30, trading-at-last to 17:40 for blue chips and mid/small caps [11] |
| Nasdaq Stockholm | Same as CET/CEST | 09:00-17:30 (unverified) | Closing auction 17:25-17:30 (unverified) |

| BME Madrid | Same as CET/CEST | 09:00-17:30 (unverified) | Closing auction 17:30-17:35 (unverified) |
| Reference: gettex (Munich, used by German brokers) | German time | 07:30-23:00 | [13] |

- **Daylight saving.** Clock changes of the EU, the UK and Switzerland fall on the same days (unverified), so the UK stays exactly one hour behind CET all year. The mismatch is with the US. By the usual rules, in 2026 the US switched on 8 March and the EU on 29 March (three mismatch weeks), and the EU switches back on 25 October versus 1 November in the US, so trading days 26–30 Oct 2026 are a mismatch week (computed, not from a source). In those weeks the US session starts at 14:30 CET instead of 15:30 CEST.
- **Lunch breaks:** none for the main equity segments (inferred from the schedules above).
- **Holidays 2026 (Euronext):** closed on all markets 1 Jan, 3 Apr (Good Friday), 6 Apr, 1 May, 25 Dec. On 24 and 31 Dec, Amsterdam, Brussels, Dublin, Lisbon and Paris have half days, Milan and Oslo are closed. Several Oslo and Dublin days differ [10]. SIX publishes a separate calendar [12]. The LSE bank-holiday calendar was not verified.
- **Price limits.** European venues generally use dynamic price collars and volatility interruptions (short auctions) instead of fixed daily limits; thresholds differ by exchange and stock (unverified). Check each rulebook.
- **Settlement.** Today T+2 for most European shares (unverified). ESMA recommended, and Regulation (EU) 2025/2075 adopted, T+1 from 11 October 2027 [15][46]. The UK and Switzerland plan the same date (unverified).
- **Lot sizes.** Most large European shares trade in single shares with no minimum lot (unverified); minimum order size is set by your broker.

## 4. Currency and FX risk for a euro investor

- Euro-area listings (Paris, Amsterdam, Brussels, Lisbon, Milan, Madrid, Helsinki, Dublin, Athens) carry no FX risk against the euro.
- Non-euro: GBP (London; many UK shares are quoted in pence, *GBX*), CHF (Zurich), SEK, DKK, NOK. ECB reference rates on 16 Oct 2026: EUR 1 = GBP 0.84385, CHF 0.9272, SEK 11.1450, DKK 7.4748, NOK 10.6240 [24]. The Danish krone is tied to the euro by an exchange-rate mechanism (ERM II) (unverified).
- Many "European" companies earn most revenue abroad, so the share price embeds currency exposure beyond the listing currency (inferred).
- If you buy in euros on a German venue, the venue converts the price; the FX risk of the underlying listing remains economically (inferred).

## 5. Regulator and investor protection

- **EU level.** ESMA is an authority of the EU [17] that coordinates supervision with the national regulators. Key rules: MiFID II (2014/65/EU: investor protection, venue and transparency rules) [18], MAR (596/2014: bans insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation; issuers disclose inside information; managers' transactions are notified) [17][19], PRIIPs (1286/2014: key information document, *KID*, for packaged products such as certificates and some funds) [20]. Application dates (MiFID II 3 Jan 2018, MAR 3 Jul 2016, PRIIPs 1 Jan 2018) are unverified.
- **National regulators** (unverified unless cited): AMF (France), AFM (Netherlands), FSMA (Belgium), CMVM (Portugal), Consob (Italy), Central Bank of Ireland, CNMV (Spain) [23], FCA (UK) [21], FINMA (Switzerland) with SIX Exchange Regulation [22], Finansinspektionen (Sweden), Finanstilsynet (Denmark, Norway), FIN-FSA (Finland). Euronext markets are supervised by their national authorities (unverified).
- **UK post-Brexit.** The UK is outside EU rules since 2021 and has its own FCA-run listing and disclosure regime (unverified). Treat UK shares as third-country securities for EU rules (inferred).
- **Filings databases.** UK: FCA National Storage Mechanism (regulatory announcements, annual financial reports, prospectuses; not real-time) [21]. Switzerland: SIX significant-shareholder database, published before 07:30 the next day [22]. Spain: CNMV registers (significant shareholdings, director dealings, annual reports) [23]. EU-wide: ESMA's ESAP collects data from July 2026 and opens to the public by July 2027 [16]. Euronext issuers publish via national channels (unverified).
- Investor-protection schemes depend on your broker (custody and compensation), not on the exchange; see [Germany](germany.md).

## 6. Market characteristics and specific risks

- **Concentration.** Banks, industrials and health care lead the EURO STOXX 50 and STOXX Europe 600 [5][6]. The FTSE 100's top 5 weigh 33 % [7]. Dependence on few mega-caps (ASML, Nestle, Novartis, AstraZeneca, HSBC) is common (unverified).
- **Volatility.** Europe's indices rose sharply in 2025 (IBEX 35 +49.3 %; SMI +14.4 %) [4]; past returns say little about the future.
- **Investor base.** Mostly institutional; retail participation varies by country (unverified).
- **Small and mid caps.** Liquidity in AIM, Euronext Growth, Nasdaq First North and BME Growth is thin (unverified); spreads on German venues can be wider than at home.
- **Political and regulatory risk.** Taxes on trades (section 8), changes of listing rules and mergers of venues (Euronext added Athens in November 2025, unverified; the June 2026 release lists Greece among its markets [1]) change costs and access.
- **Dividends.** Withholding tax drag differs by country (section 8).

## 7. Access from Germany via Trade Republic

- Trade Republic lists assets in its app ("All assets" browser) [45]. Whether a given European share is tradable, on which venue (typically a German partner venue or market maker) and in which currency is **not** confirmed by the sources reached; check in the app (unverified). Orders on partner venues are typically in EUR (unverified).
- **Why European stocks also trade on German venues.** Frankfurt's Xetra offers German and international stocks [14]; gettex lists shares and an "Europe 50" index and trades 07:30-23:00 [13], much longer than the home exchanges. Euro quoting, extended hours and broker-friendly execution are the likely reasons (inferred). Taxes such as UK stamp duty or French FTT apply by issuer (inferred, unverified), not by venue.
- **UCITS ETF categories (examples only, not recommendations; availability must be checked in the app):**
  - Europe broad: iShares Core MSCI Europe UCITS ETF EUR (Acc), ISIN IE00B4K48X80, tracks MSCI Europe, accumulating [42].
  - STOXX Europe 600: Xtrackers STOXX Europe 600 UCITS ETF 1C, LU0328475792, accumulating [43].
  - Euro area blue chips: iShares EURO STOXX 50 UCITS ETF (DE), DE0005933956, distributing [44].
  - Single-country and sector UCITS ETFs for UK, Switzerland and others exist (unverified). Equity ETFs benefit from a 30 % partial exemption (*Teilfreistellung*) for private investors in Germany (see [Trading basics](../01-trading-basics.md#funds-and-etfs)).
- **ADRs / secondary listings.** Some European companies also trade in the US as ADRs (unverified). For a euro investor these add USD risk and US venue taxes; the local line is usually cheaper (inferred).

## 8. Taxes specific to this market

**Trade taxes (buyer):**
- UK: buyers usually pay 0.5 % stamp duty reserve tax (SDRT) on electronic purchases; 1.5 % in some depositary-receipt cases [25]. Exemptions (AIM shares, ETFs) were not verified.
- France: *taxe sur les transactions financières* (TTF) of 0.4 % on large caps (above EUR 1 bn) (unverified). Italy and Spain also tax share purchases (rate unverified).
- Switzerland: *Umsatzabgabe* (securities transfer stamp) applies only via Swiss securities dealers (unverified).

**Dividend withholding tax (WHT), German-resident individual** [PwC secondary; check official sources]:

| Country | Domestic WHT | Treaty rate with Germany (portfolio) | Excess to reclaim |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | None [35] (REIT property income 20 % [35]) | n/a | none |
| Switzerland | 35 % [30] | 15 % [30] | 20 points, from ESTV [27] |
| France | 12.8 % for individuals [31] | 15 % [31] | none if 12.8 % is applied; a higher rate withheld at source is reclaimed (unverified) |
| Netherlands | 15 % (unverified) | 15 % [32] | none |
| Spain | 19 % [33] | 15 % [33] | 4 points |
| Italy | 26 % [34] | 10 or 15 % [34] | up to 11/26 reimbursable [34] |
| Sweden | 30 % [36] | 15 % [36] | 15 points |
| Denmark | 27 % [37] | 15 % [37] | 12 points |
| Norway | 25 % [38] | 15 % [38] | 10 points |
| Finland | 30-35 % [39] | 15 % [39] | 15-20 points |
| Ireland | 25 %, exempt for EU-resident individuals with a declaration [40] | 15 % (unverified) | via declaration |
| Belgium | 30 % [41] | 15 % [41] | 15 points |

- **Credit in Germany.** The *Abgeltungsteuer* is 25 % [29]. Foreign tax is credited against it, up to 25 % of each dividend, and only for the part not subject to a reduction claim [28]. So you can credit about the treaty rate and must reclaim the excess from the source country.
- **Swiss reclaim.** Swiss WHT is final for foreign residents unless a treaty gives a refund [26]. German residents can file via the ESTV portal VST-DE or on paper; the claim lapses after three years from the end of the calendar year in which the dividend became due; yearly filing, with income statements or tax vouchers attached [27].
- Whether Trade Republic withholds the full domestic rate or the treaty rate, and what documents it provides, must be checked (open question).
- Tax is highly individual; consult a tax advisor.
