# Strategy library

Three strategies are executable. All remaining entries are research specifications, not enabled
controls or demonstrated profitable systems. Configurations are illustrative; no personal capital,
risk tolerance, horizon or security recommendation is inferred.

## Executable strategies

| Name / factory key | Hypothesis | Rules | Data and schedule | Main rejection conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal-weight buy-and-hold / `buy_hold` | Broad ownership supplies a simple reference for active rules | Allocate equally among listings with completed data once; retain positions and residual cash | Registered raw closes, explicit calendars, FX, actions; first available decision, next scheduled opens | Missing valuation/FX, limits breached, unresolved action/delisting |
| Moving-average trend / `trend` | Persistent price trends might distinguish risk-on periods from cash periods | Latest split-normalized local close strictly above trailing SMA; equal weight active listings, otherwise cash | Complete lookback (default 60 completed sessions); first available monthly cutoff | Incomplete warm-up stays cash; calendar outage rejects; unknown/late action rejects |
| Cross-sectional momentum / `momentum` | Relatively strong positive local total-return trends might persist | Trailing local gross total return strictly positive; select top N (default 3), stable instrument ID tie-break; equal weights | Lookback+1 completed observations (default 61); monthly cutoff; action-safe return index | Incomplete warm-up stays cash; unknown dividend/split chronology or calendar outage rejects |

All use the same EUR ledger, costs, freshness, concentration, order-size and prior-volume limits.
Risk caps can reduce allocations below their nominal equal weights. No shorting, leverage, stop-loss,
intraday stop, or automated tax withholding is modeled. Residual EUR cash earns zero. Selling a position
does not erase a dividend entitlement already earned. A new monthly signal cannot change a previous fill.

### Point-in-time signals

Trend divides earlier raw closes by the product of splits that have become effective and known by
the decision, putting its trailing SMA and latest close in the same share units. A neutral two-for-one
split therefore cannot create a trend signal. Dividend price gaps remain price effects in this
price-trend rule.

Momentum uses a local gross-return index. Between two completed closes, begin with one prior unit,
apply known split ratios and cash entitlements in event order, then divide the ending value plus
dividend cash by the prior close. The index assumes gross dividend reinvestment at the next completed
close for **ranking only**. Actual portfolio accounting instead retains receivables until exact
payment, uses EUR FX and subtracts actual modeled costs. Ranking performance is not account performance.
Same-time post-split dividends use the declared split-adjusted entitlement.

Only actions already effective and available may normalize a signal. An unknown late action is not
silently used to repair earlier decisions. A missing expected session is not dropped to make a
lookback appear complete. The engine owns scheduling; strategy instances carry no run history, so
the same configuration can be rerun or compared reproducibly.

### Parameters, turnover and failure regimes

For experiments, lookbacks such as 20, 60, 120 and 252 completed observations and top N from 1 to the
selected universe size define a **research grid**, not recommended settings. The implementation accepts
integer lookbacks of at least 2 and top N of at least 1. No grid was optimized for the supplied sample.
Smaller lookbacks can increase churn; larger lookbacks can lag regime changes. Monthly signals can miss
abrupt reversals between decisions.

Buy-and-hold can experience sustained losses and concentration drift and has no protective exit.
Trend can repeatedly buy high and sell low in sideways markets. Momentum can reverse sharply, cluster
by sector/country, or select low-liquidity listings. Local-currency rankings can diverge from EUR
results when FX moves. Equal nominal weights are not equal risk allocations.

Transaction charges, spread/slippage, next-open gaps and participation caps can reverse gross
advantages. Sizes reserve current cash only, so rotation proceeds may remain cash until the next
monthly decision. Maximum position weight caps the **post-trade** bought position, not future drift
from price movement. Declining to buy because of an execution cap is an observable outcome, not a
reason to change limits until the chart improves.

Evaluate every configuration against `buy_hold` under the same dates, costs, limits, capital,
available universe and lag. Reject a research claim when it depends on future data, action mistakes,
stale marks, an artificial calendar, ignored fees, or a hand-picked favorable period.

## Research specifications — not executable

| Approach | Rationale / hypothesis | Required data and proposed rule | Sizing, risk, turnover and failures | Evidence needed before activation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Periodic rebalancing | Restore a declared allocation after relative moves | Explicit allocation, monthly or annual rebalance; the allocation wrapper supports once/monthly only | Same caps/costs; sells can leave cash pending the next decision; contributions deferred | Compare frequency and costs without optimizing the evaluation period |
| Time-series momentum | Own assets with positive own trailing return | Action-safe return per asset; predefined horizon and monthly rule | Equal/risk-based allocation subject to caps; whipsaw and correlated reversals | Separate from cross-sectional ranking; walk-forward test and consistent benchmark |
| Mean reversion | Transitory deviations may reverse | Stable definition of deviation, entries/exits and corporate-action handling | Tight liquidity/spread controls; potentially high turnover; trends can overwhelm thesis | Realistic gap/stop handling, out-of-sample evidence and cost stress |
| Sector relative strength | Sector leadership might persist | Point-in-time sector membership and comparable total-return series | Sector concentration constraints; classification changes and narrow leadership | Historical membership, survivorship controls and broad benchmark |
| Volatility targeting | Reduce exposure when measured volatility rises | Past realized volatility only; explicit leverage-free target and lag | Cap at cash-funded exposure; high turnover in stress, volatility lag | Stable estimator, gap stress and turnover sensitivity |
| Risk parity / inverse volatility | Avoid nominal equal weights dominating risk | Aligned calendars, covariance or trailing vol, stable positive inputs | No leverage; unstable covariance and concentration in low-vol assets | Singular-input treatment, rebalance stability and genuine multi-asset data |
| Commodity-related trend | Commodity cycles may persist | Distinguish futures total returns, roll yield, ETCs and mining equities | Contract/product-specific currency and concentration limits; roll and equity basis risk | Eligible instruments, licensed series, roll/carry and action semantics |
| Currency trend/carry | Trends or interest differentials may persist | FX spot plus point-in-time rates and executable financing assumptions | Foreign cash and leverage not supported by current EUR-cash ledger | Correct carry/roll accounting, accessible products and separate engine scope |
| Multi-asset macro | Economic regimes may affect asset groups | Vintage-safe macro releases, publication lags, predetermined regime rules | Diversification can disappear in stress; low sample size and many choices | ALFRED-like vintages, chronological evaluation and multiple-testing controls |
| Factor equity screening | Valuation/profitability/etc. may relate to returns | Point-in-time filings, membership, delistings, accounting dates | Sector/country/size neutralization decisions; crowded-factor and revision risk | Publication-aware data and explicit investable universe |
| Event-driven framework | Defined public events may alter future returns | Timestamped event source, eligibility, entry/exit and lag | Event overlap, spreads and selection effects; stops not modeled | Auditable event timestamps, matched baseline and leakage tests |

An allocation simulation is implemented as a wrapper around the same engine; that does not make
external contribution schedules, risk-parity optimization or currency carry implemented.

## Regional hypotheses

Possible research comparisons include US growth versus European value; Japanese exporters and yen;
semiconductor chains across US/Taiwan/Korea; Indonesian commodity exporters; Chinese consumer demand;
European industrials and energy prices; and copper versus industrial activity. These are questions,
not signals. They require verified access, point-in-time constituents, currency attribution and source
licensing. The nine generic demo listings cannot answer those questions.

## Actual tests and limitations

The 2026-10-20 financial tests verified neutral split signals, dividend-inclusive momentum, physically
filtered histories, next-open execution and invariance of an earlier fill to later HLCV mutations.
`tests/financial_demo.py` produced all three downloadable synthetic reports with costs, actions and
rejections intact. `docs/BACKTEST_METHODOLOGY.md` defines the exact statistical and execution rules.

No real-data profitability test, holdout, walk-forward, parameter robustness claim or Monte Carlo
result is supplied. Before interpreting a real-data strategy, fix the hypothesis and parameters,
reserve chronological holdouts, record every attempted configuration, stress costs and missing data,
and review independent failures. Passing software tests is not evidence of future investment returns.
