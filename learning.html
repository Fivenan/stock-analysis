# Trading guide: understanding, testing and paper practice

This course uses EUR accounts and invented numbers. It teaches questions and reproducible experiments, not an expected return or a recommendation. Use market access, providers, broker research, tax/regulation and the dated source register for externally sourced claims. Documentation covers more markets and instruments than the executable sample supports. Current fees, eligibility, taxation and entitlements must be checked against the linked official source before any real decision.

## Beginner: ownership, venues and prices

A share is a residual ownership claim; a bond is a contractual debt claim with maturity, default and rate risk. A fund pools assets; an ETF trades on a venue and may differ from its net asset value. Index exposure is a rule-based basket, not necessarily a product available to you. Currency is both a quotation unit and a risk factor. Derivatives reference an underlying and can embed leverage, expiry, margin and nonlinear losses. The local app does not model them.

A stock exchange matches orders under venue rules. The highest bid is what a buyer currently offers; the lowest ask is what a seller requests. A EUR100 bid and EUR100.20 ask imply a EUR0.20 spread, about 20 basis points of midpoint EUR100.10. A round trip crossing both sides loses roughly EUR0.20 per share before fees if nothing moves. Displayed depth is quantity at successive prices; it can disappear. Volume is completed trades, not a promise you can transact that volume at one price. Thin overseas hours can have wider spreads than overlapping home-market hours. A broker's reference-market chart is not a fill guarantee.

A market order prioritizes execution, not price. A limit order constrains price but may never fill. A stop triggers an order after a threshold and can gap beyond it. A stop-limit can trigger yet remain unfilled. The app simulates daily next-open market orders only; its spread/slippage assumptions are not a real order book. Session calendars, time zones and daylight-saving changes govern which information is actually available. Settlement happens after execution under the instrument/venue rules; a backtest that immediately reuses proceeds makes a simplifying cash assumption. Confirm actual broker settlement and trading restrictions separately.

Price changes reflect both cash-flow expectations and required returns. A company can grow profits while its share falls if expectations were higher or discount rates rise. Earnings surprises, guidance, capital structure, competition, liquidity and regulation interact. A thesis such as “semiconductor demand will grow” lacks entry, exit, valuation, instrument, timing and falsification rules. A strategy must specify all of these before testing and can still fail despite a correct economic story.

## Beginner: returns, corporate actions and EUR translation

Simple return is `r = (ending value + distributions − starting value) / starting value`. Log return is `ln(1+r)`; log returns add over time while simple returns compound. +10% then −10% gives `1.1 × 0.9 − 1 = −1%`, not zero. Log returns are undefined at −100% and must not conceal bankruptcy.

A EUR account holding USD assets has both local price and exchange-rate exposure. Define `f` as EUR per one USD, never the inverse. EUR return before costs is `(P1/P0) × (f1/f0) − 1`. USD100 rises to USD110 while f falls from 0.90 to 0.81: EUR90 becomes EUR89.10, so return is −1%, despite +10% local price return. A security trading in EUR may still own foreign businesses; quotation currency and underlying economic exposure differ.

Dividend example: one share marked EUR100 becomes EUR99 at ex date with EUR1 entitlement. Equity remains EUR100 if the dividend receivable is recognized. Payment changes receivable to cash, not another EUR1 gain. A sale between ex and payment does not erase the recorded entitlement. Tax/withholding and conversion fees can alter real outcomes; this engine models zero dividend FX fee and no tax withholding. Adjusted total-return prices plus separately credited dividends double-count returns.

Split example: one share at EUR100 becomes two at EUR50 in a 2-for-1 split. Quantity doubles, per-share basis halves, total basis and equity remain EUR100. Raw close returns show a mechanical −50%; action-safe research returns do not. The app exposes raw prices and a separate action-safe index. Delisting is different: unavailable quotes do not establish zero loss or a recoverable terminal value. Unknown proceeds must stop a trustworthy valuation.

Cash accounting example: EUR1,000 buys 10 shares at USD100 with f=0.90. Consideration is EUR900 and cash becomes EUR100 before costs. A EUR1 fixed fee plus EUR0.45 FX fee makes cash EUR98.55. If the next mark remains USD100 but f becomes 1.00, assets are EUR1,000 and total equity EUR1,098.55. FX gain is not a local stock gain. Buys consume consideration and costs; sells receive proceeds less their own costs. FIFO realized P&L allocates acquisition and disposal costs to matched lots. Open lots have unrealized gains, not completed winning trades.

## Intermediate: analyzing a business and an economy

Read income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement together. Revenue growth with worsening receivables can differ from cash generation. Operating income excludes financing; net income includes it. Free cash flow is often approximated as operating cash flow minus capital expenditure, but definitions and accounting adjustments must be documented. Net debt, interest coverage, dilution, pension obligations and off-balance commitments affect ownership value. A low P/E can reflect cyclical peak earnings or structural decline rather than cheapness.

A discounted cash-flow estimate is `V = Σ CF_t/(1+k)^t + terminal value/(1+k)^T`. EUR10 each year for two years at 10% has present value `10/1.1 + 10/1.1² = EUR17.36`. Terminal assumptions often dominate a long-horizon estimate; show a grid of growth/discount-rate cases instead of a precise target. Enterprise value and equity value differ by financing claims. Compare multiples only across compatible accounting, leverage, business models and cycles. Fundamental data must carry publication dates; fiscal period end is not the day investors knew the number.

Technical analysis summarizes price/volume behavior. A trailing SMA is the mean of the last L completed closes. Trend rules hypothesize persistence; momentum ranks past returns; mean reversion hypothesizes reversal after deviation. Indicators are transforms of existing observations, not independent evidence. Adding five correlated indicators rarely supplies five independent reasons. A moving average crossing after the close cannot execute at that same close in this engine. Split normalization must use only actions effective and available by the decision time.

Macroeconomic relationships are conditional. Central-bank tightening can raise discount rates, slow lending and support a currency, yet an already-anticipated hike can produce the opposite immediate price response. Nominal rates approximately combine real rates and expected inflation; bond prices generally fall as required yields rise, with duration approximating local sensitivity `ΔP/P ≈ −D × Δyield`. A duration-6 instrument facing a one percentage point yield increase has approximately −6% price sensitivity before convexity. This is educational, not an implemented bond model.

Business cycles affect sectors through demand, inventories, financing and pricing power. Rotation and factor exposures (value, size, quality, momentum) can remain out of favor for years. Cross-market correlation is estimated, unstable and often rises during stress. Commodity price analysis needs supply, demand, inventories, capacity, substitution and transport constraints. Physical metal, futures, an ETC/ETN, a mining share and a mining ETF have different storage, roll, issuer, operational and equity risks. Nickel exposure through an Indonesian exporter adds country, currency and company risk; it does not replicate nickel spot returns. China home listings, Hong Kong listings and depositary receipts have different access and legal structures. Read the access matrix rather than assuming a data symbol is tradable.

Geopolitical risk can interrupt custody, settlement, ownership rights or capital conversion. A broad diversified label does not remove common suppliers, energy prices or policy exposure. An earnings forecast and a macro forecast should be expressed as ranges with observable rejection conditions: e.g. lower volumes, inventory build, financing deterioration or a policy change. Avoid substituting a compelling narrative for measured data.

## Intermediate: statistics and portfolio construction

For daily returns `r_i`, arithmetic mean is `Σr_i/n`; sample variance is `Σ(r_i−mean)²/(n−1)`. Annualized sample volatility uses `std × sqrt(252)` here, a disclosed convention rather than a universal count of trading days. Geometric annual return uses elapsed calendar time: `(E_end/E_start)^(365.25/days)−1`. Arithmetic mean ×252 is different and can overstate compound growth. Standard deviation treats gains/losses symmetrically and is incomplete for tail risk.

Covariance measures joint deviations; correlation divides covariance by the product of standard deviations. Portfolio variance is `wᵀΣw`. Two 50% positions each with 20% volatility and zero correlation have volatility `sqrt(.25×.04 + .25×.04) = 14.14%`; if correlation is one, it is 20%. These inputs are estimates, not guarantees. Covariance matrices estimated from a short, changing history can make optimized weights unstable. Constraints and simple baselines often expose more useful research questions than unconstrained optimization.

Position sizing must account for equity, concentration, liquidity and plausible losses. If a hypothetical EUR10,000 policy allows EUR100 loss on a EUR5 per-share stress, naive size is 20 shares before costs. A stop does not guarantee the EUR5 maximum, and a gap can exceed it. Apply a separate notional cap and liquidity cap, then test the combined worst case. The app enforces long-only position and order/volume limits; it does not execute stop-based risk sizing. Choose your own policy values rather than copying this example.

Drawdown is `equity/running peak − 1`. EUR100→80 is −20%; recovery requires +25%, not +20%. Duration measures time underwater and may remain unrecovered at the endpoint. Sharpe here is mean daily excess / sample daily excess std ×sqrt(252). Sortino uses the root mean squared negative excess returns across all observations. Calmar is CAGR / absolute maximum drawdown. Flat equity can make denominators undefined: “None” with an explanation is more truthful than an infinite score. Winning-trade percentage excludes open FIFO lots and does not imply profit if losing trades are much larger. Two-sided turnover here is absolute traded EUR / (2 × mean equity). Inspect the methodology before comparing metrics from another platform.

## Advanced: designing a falsifiable experiment

Write a hypothesis before choosing parameters: “positive trailing local total-return momentum among the specified universe may persist enough after EUR costs to beat a same-cost equal-weight benchmark.” Freeze universe eligibility, lookback, ranking tie-break, top N, observation timing, monthly schedule, execution lag, weights, fees and reject conditions. Record an experiment ID and source hashes. A synthetic run verifies plumbing; it cannot test that economic hypothesis.

Survivorship bias occurs when today's surviving instruments replace the historically available universe. Look-ahead bias occurs when a future value or publication affects an earlier decision. Revised macro data needs vintages: today's revised GDP is not what a historical trader knew. Selection bias arises from choosing markets or start dates after seeing results. Delisted assets, missing sessions, FX direction errors, dividend double counting and split-induced signals can dominate the supposed edge. Do not fill missing execution prices with future values. Old marks can be flagged valuations within limits, not executable prices.

Train/validation/test periods must be chronological, with leakage-aware separation where overlapping labels are used. Use training to define a candidate, validation to choose among planned alternatives and an untouched test once for evaluation. Repeatedly consulting the test set converts it into training. Walk-forward refits only on past data then evaluates later segments; expanding windows and rolling windows encode different assumptions about regime persistence. These are planned research features, not implemented app buttons. A written manual research protocol is not evidence that automated OOS validation occurred.

Multiple testing: if 100 independent ineffective rules each have a 5% false-positive chance, the chance of at least one apparent success is `1−0.95^100 ≈ 99.4%`. Tests are often correlated, but the selection problem remains. Register the number of tried rules, include failures and consider appropriate corrections before a claim. A p-value measures data incompatibility with a specified null under assumptions, not the probability a strategy is profitable. Economic magnitude, costs, dependence, confidence intervals and replication matter.

Regression `return = α + β×market + error` can distinguish broad market exposure from residual returns only under its sample/model assumptions. Autocorrelation invalidates naive independent-observation standard errors. Nonstationarity means distributions or relationships change over time. Apparent regimes defined after the outcome are another form of leakage. Fat tails, skew and volatility clustering make a normal-return model an incomplete stress model. Resample-based or Monte Carlo analysis must declare its dependence assumptions; shuffling daily returns can destroy important path structure. This app does not implement such methods or claim calibrated probabilities.

Machine learning adds model capacity and opportunities for leakage. Fit scalers/imputers on training only; align features by their real publication time; purge overlapping labels where needed; compare against simple baselines after the same costs. Feature importance is not causal proof. Reject a model that needs future revisions, cannot survive modest cost changes, relies on a single year/listing, or cannot be reproduced from immutable inputs. A sensitivity surface with a broad stable region is more informative than one best point, but remains in-sample if evaluated on reused data.

## Exercises and worked solutions

1. EUR translation: a JPY share rises 8%, while EUR per JPY falls 5%. Solution: `1.08×.95−1 = 2.6%` before costs, not 3% exactly.
2. Split/dividend: two shares at EUR60 split 3-for-1, then pay EUR0.50 per post-split share. Raw price after ex is EUR19.50. Solution: six shares worth EUR117 plus EUR3 entitlement = EUR120. Cash payment transfers EUR3 from receivable to cash. Total basis remains EUR120 before any other changes.
3. Costs: buy five units at EUR40, fixed fee EUR1, commission 10 bps. Solution: consideration EUR200, commission EUR0.20, total cash debit EUR201.20 before spread/slippage. Do not subtract those fees again from end equity.
4. Compounding: +20%, −20%, +5% from EUR1,000. Solution: `1000×1.2×.8×1.05 = EUR1008`; +0.8% total. Average simple return is 1.667% per period and is not the compound result.
5. Concentration: EUR3,000 in one listing, EUR2,000 in another and EUR5,000 cash. Solution: total equity EUR10,000, position weights 30%/20%, invested exposure 50%. Country weights may sum to 50% when cash is separately shown. Cash belongs in the denominator.
6. Drawdown: EUR100→120→90→110. Solution: maximum drawdown `90/120−1 = −25%`, still unrecovered at EUR110. A 33.33% gain from EUR90 is needed to regain EUR120.
7. Leakage: use an earnings report released March 10 to trade March 1 because its period ended December 31. Solution: invalid; align availability to March 10 (or later actual public availability), then use the next allowable execution event.
8. Order replay: submit PAPER-X, restart and submit the same payload. Solution: one durable fill, returned again. Change quantity under PAPER-X: reject canonical conflict. New intention requires a new ID and nondecreasing simulation time.
9. Hypothesis audit: a momentum rule wins only with zero fees in one selected country/year. Solution: reject a robustness/profitability claim; document selection, rerun planned costs and untouched periods with lawful real data. The synthetic app cannot repair the evidentiary gap.
10. Policy exercise: fill every IPS blank and invent two stress scenarios, including one correlated price/FX loss. Solution rubric: values must be user supplied, internally consistent and within cash/liquidity needs; passing is a documented review, not adopting any numeric template here.

## Glossary

Alpha: residual return relative to a specified model. Ask/bid: lowest sell/highest buy quote. Basis point: 0.01 percentage point. Benchmark: declared comparison portfolio. CAGR: compounded annual growth by elapsed time. Corporate action: event changing ownership/cash entitlement. Covariance: joint deviations from means. Drawdown: decline from prior equity peak. Ex date: entitlement boundary. Exposure: invested value relative to equity. FIFO: first acquired lot matched first. FX: currency conversion. Liquidity: ability to trade size at acceptable cost. MIC: venue identifier. Momentum: persistence hypothesis based on past return. NAV: fund net asset value. Overfitting: fitting sample noise that fails later. Point in time: only information available by the decision. Provenance: source, retrieval and immutable identity. Receivable: owned payment claim not yet cash. Rebalance: restore target weights. Regime: hypothesized state of market dynamics. Slippage: execution difference beyond declared reference. Spread: ask minus bid. Survivorship: omission of failed/delisted instruments. TWR: chain-linked return excluding external-flow effects. UCITS/PRIIPs/KID: regulatory/product concepts requiring instrument-specific official checks. Volatility: dispersion of returns under a stated convention.

## A first experiment

1. Open Global Dashboard. Change the watchlist and read country, currency and source fields. None of the DEMO IDs is a real ticker or an availability assertion.
2. Market Explorer filters the registered country, sector, currency, class and venue. Empty filters mean all currently remaining rows.
3. Asset Research selects a listing and a date interval. Raw prices are execution inputs. The separate local gross return index uses the engine's point-in-time split/dividend helper, so the US split is not reported as a 50% investment loss. FX and costs are excluded from that educational index; EUR account returns are in backtests.
4. Strategy Builder or Backtesting Lab selects one of three active strategies. Set universe, dates, lookback, top N, initial EUR capital, costs and limits. Buy-hold ignores lookback/top N. Trend needs the lookback number of observations; momentum needs one additional observation. Earlier data provides warm-up; only the configured run dates are traded.
5. Run backtest and benchmark. The benchmark uses the same universe, dates, capital, costs and execution rules. Read warnings before metrics. No fills can be a valid consequence of limits, not a software crash. Low nominal local prices in several synthetic currencies lead to volume-cap rejections; these are deliberately visible.
6. Download JSON for configuration, provenance, metrics, warnings, equity and fills. CSV exports separate equity and fills. Save experiment writes a durable manifest. Select repository IDs under Compare saved experiments; each shows parameters, metrics and equity. The repository ID differs from the engine experiment hash.

Initial capital/cost/limit defaults are illustrative. Do not interpret EUR10,000 or a 30% position cap as advice. The engine permits long-only unleveraged cash equity/ETF-like units; no shorting, derivatives, leverage or stops are implemented. Aggregate weights cannot exceed one. Remaining weight is EUR cash with zero interest.

## Portfolio and risk

Portfolio Simulator takes listing weights as decimal fractions, e.g. 0.10 means 10%, and once/monthly rebalancing. It uses the same engine and defaults as backtesting, including costs and rejection rules. Weights must sum to at most one. Contributions and TWR are deferred: do not treat this as a savings-plan projection.

Risk Dashboard values the durable paper account at its simulation cursor and includes cash, positions and dividend receivables. Currency buckets include all three. Country/sector buckets include securities and attributable receivables, with cash separate; total equity is the denominator. Price shocks affect positions; foreign FX shocks affect foreign positions and receivables. These are instantaneous arithmetic scenarios, not probabilities, VaR or forecasts. Latest in-session backtest metrics supply experiment drawdown. No risk budget is personalized until the user defines one.

The tax control is an isolated estimate `max(0, gain − remaining allowance) × supplied effective rate`. Enter your own scenario inputs; there is no legal default. It neither withholds cash nor imports broker tax records. Fund exemptions, loss baskets, foreign withholding, church-tax rules and advance lump sums are excluded. Consult the sourced tax guide for scope and current official documents.

## Durable internal paper practice

Paper Trading asks you to initialize a new private account explicitly with configurable initial capital (illustrative default EUR10,000), then retains that capital on restart. Risk and Journal pages ask you to initialize first rather than silently creating an account. It displays the saved capital and cursor. To study a separate account, stop the app and launch with a separate `GMRL_RUNTIME`; never erase a prior account to hide losses. It cannot connect to a broker.

Select a historical decision date (UTC end of day), listing, buy/sell and positive quantity. A manual order is attempted at the next scheduled historical opening, with current configured default costs/limits. One unit DEMO-DE on January 2, 2024 is a convenient small sample. Press Submit simulated order. Repeating the same button with the same PAPER ID and unchanged payload returns the same fill: reruns do not buy twice. For a genuinely new order, press New order ID first. Reusing an ID with a changed quantity, dataset or settings rejects. New operations cannot precede the saved simulation cursor; identical replay still succeeds. An end-of-sample decision has no next opening and rejects.

Advance actions processes known dividend/split events and payments to the selected historical cutoff without inventing a trade. It may reject backward or unavailable cutoffs. Orders are whole manual quantities or reject; insufficient cash including fees, excess concentration, excessive volume, stale FX, sell quantity and split-between-decision/open are actionable errors. Account snapshot, canonical orders, fills and action records remain visible.

Trade Journal appends hypothesis, entry/exit plan, lesson and optional PAPER link. Revisions are new entries referencing the previous entry ID in the text. Prior rows stay intact. Export journal JSON privately; it is never included in the static build. After restarting the app, revisit Paper Trading and Trade Journal to confirm the same durable state.

## Troubleshooting and recovery

No observations: choose dates in 2024–2025 and a nonempty universe. Reversed bounds: correct start/end. No fills: inspect warm-up, concentration/volume limits, costs and order-cancellation warnings rather than weakening checks for attractive returns. A paper time error: select a later decision and a new ID or replay the exact old payload. Account-capital conflicts: retain the original capital; use a separate private runtime for a separate scenario. Close other app processes before backing up `.runtime` with native file copy. Restore the complete directory together, not individual SQLite files during writes. Keep backups and exports private. There is no network dependency in ordinary sample use.

## Common adapter and identity contracts — recommended design

Adapters should expose capability metadata separately from operations: instruments/series supported, data kind, intervals, earliest/latest history if known, adjustment mode, delay, key/entitlement requirements, quota units and rights tags. Operations may include `fetch_bars`, `fetch_fx`, `fetch_macro`, `fetch_actions` and `healthcheck`; unsupported operations must fail clearly. An annual macro response must not satisfy the daily-bar interface merely because both contain numbers and dates.

Use central instrument identity plus listing/provider mappings: internal ID, ISIN where applicable, MIC, local ticker, legal asset type, quote currency, unit/multiplier, provider symbol and evidence/validity dates. Never derive a TR listing from a Yahoo-style suffix. Mark illustrative mappings unverified and block real-price joins until checked. Separate ordinary shares, ADRs, ETFs, ETCs/ETNs, futures contracts, currencies and macro series.

Every returned record needs source/provider/endpoint, retrieval timestamp (UTC), observation/session date and timezone, units/currency, adjustment mode, data status (actual/delayed/synthetic/missing/estimated), release/availability time where known and validation outcome. For macro data distinguish observation period, publication date, retrieval date and vintage. Missing availability time blocks claims of point-in-time backtest validity.

## Cache, incremental ingestion and quality — recommended design

1. Canonical request key: provider + endpoint + normalized instrument/series + interval + requested range + adjustment mode + relevant version. Never cache credentials in keys/logs.
2. Store immutable raw response hashes/metadata if rights allow; normalized columnar Parquet for bars and SQLite/DuckDB metadata/query layer as environment permits. Cache format choice is not proof of historical accuracy. Do not claim Parquet if only CSV exists.
3. Increment from last validated observation with a small overlap for corrections; retain revision history or snapshots. Do not assume newest observations are immutable. Backfill corporate actions and delistings separately.
4. Configure TTL by data product: illustrative EOD cache 24h after expected publication, FX reference cache until next publication, annual macro days rather than seconds, reference mappings weekly with explicit expiry. These are design defaults, not provider guarantees.
5. Validate schema, finite/positive required prices, ordering, duplicate dates, OHLC relations, units/multipliers, expected sessions, missing values, extreme jumps and corporate-action consistency. Never forward-fill a non-trading day into an executable fill without explicit model policy.
6. Pick one total-return policy: raw prices plus explicit distributions/actions, or a documented adjusted series with consistent accounting. Do not add cash dividends on top of an already dividend-adjusted total-return series. Verify provider adjustment semantics rather than assuming `adj_close` always means the same thing.
7. Record quality flags and cross-check overlaps between legally usable sources. Do not silently splice providers with different venue, close time or adjustment policy. Flag discontinuities and retain provenance.

## Quotas and retries — recommended design

Maintain separate counters for HTTP calls, provider credits/weights, symbols/subscriptions, byte budgets and any per-minute/day quotas. Reservation is atomic before dispatch; reconcile charge semantics from actual provider documentation. Persist counters across app refresh/restart when required. Unknown quota/credit/reset must not become zero-cost/unlimited.

Use a token bucket or equivalent for each applicable window and an independent daily allowance. Honor Retry-After and documented reset timezone; otherwise configure a conservative boundary and expose uncertainty. Exponential backoff with jitter and maximum attempts for transient network/429/5xx errors; no retry loop on invalid keys, forbidden licenses, bad symbols or exhausted daily allowance. Count retry attempts in the request log; don't assume failed calls cost nothing. Add connection/read timeouts, circuit breaker and user-visible stale-cache fallback. No key rotation/account multiplication to evade quotas.

Batch only where the endpoint and license permit. One request can contain many symbols and still consume per-symbol weighted credits. Conditional requests and cached metadata can reduce transfer; a 304 does not automatically imply no quota charge. Disable connectors when current terms or returned entitlement errors contradict stored assumptions.

does not erase a dividend entitlement already earned. A new monthly signal cannot change a previous fill.

### Point-in-time signals

Trend divides earlier raw closes by the product of splits that have become effective and known by
the decision, putting its trailing SMA and latest close in the same share units. A neutral two-for-one
split therefore cannot create a trend signal. Dividend price gaps remain price effects in this
price-trend rule.

Momentum uses a local gross-return index. Between two completed closes, begin with one prior unit,
apply known split ratios and cash entitlements in event order, then divide the ending value plus
dividend cash by the prior close. The index assumes gross dividend reinvestment at the next completed
close for **ranking only**. Actual portfolio accounting instead retains receivables until exact
payment, uses EUR FX and subtracts actual modeled costs. Ranking performance is not account performance.
Same-time post-split dividends use the declared split-adjusted entitlement.

Only actions already effective and available may normalize a signal. An unknown late action is not
silently used to repair earlier decisions. A missing expected session is not dropped to make a
lookback appear complete. The engine owns scheduling; strategy instances carry no run history, so
the same configuration can be rerun or compared reproducibly.

### Parameters, turnover and failure regimes

For experiments, lookbacks such as 20, 60, 120 and 252 completed observations and top N from 1 to the
selected universe size define a **research grid**, not recommended settings. The implementation accepts
integer lookbacks of at least 2 and top N of at least 1. No grid was optimized for the supplied sample.
Smaller lookbacks can increase churn; larger lookbacks can lag regime changes. Monthly signals can miss
abrupt reversals between decisions.

Buy-and-hold can experience sustained losses and concentration drift and has no protective exit.
Trend can repeatedly buy high and sell low in sideways markets. Momentum can reverse sharply, cluster
by sector/country, or select low-liquidity listings. Local-currency rankings can diverge from EUR
results when FX moves. Equal nominal weights are not equal risk allocations.

Transaction charges, spread/slippage, next-open gaps and participation caps can reverse gross
advantages. Sizes reserve current cash only, so rotation proceeds may remain cash until the next
monthly decision. Maximum position weight caps the **post-trade** bought position, not future drift
from price movement. Declining to buy because of an execution cap is an observable outcome, not a
reason to change limits until the chart improves.

Evaluate every configuration against `buy_hold` under the same dates, costs, limits, capital,
available universe and lag. Reject a research claim when it depends on future data, action mistakes,
stale marks, an artificial calendar, ignored fees, or a hand-picked favorable period.

BACKTEST METHODOLOGY

# Backtest methodology and financial API

Implementation and native-Windows verification: 2026-10-20. This is an educational research engine using explicit historical or **SYNTHETIC SAMPLE** datasets. It does not connect to a broker, infer current prices, optimize for target returns, or establish investment suitability. The demonstration calendar and arithmetic prices are intentionally artificial.

## Event and information model

Each listing owns explicit sessions, raw bars, currency and availability. The engine forms a UTC union of scheduled openings, action dates, payment dates and one end-of-day cutoff on each supported session date. It retains a separate initial-capital row immediately before the requested UTC start. It never invents weekend bars or forward-fills OHLC/volume. Dates outside the configured range cannot produce trades. A requested session crossing the UTC run boundaries is rejected explicitly.

Equal-time processing is entitlement, split, payment, opening fills, then completed-bar information and decisions/valuation. An action and its identifier are applied once per simulation. Late-known actions reject validation. A simultaneous split/dividend requires declared pre-split or post-split entitlement. Unsupported fractional split units and cash-in-lieu reject rather than round away property.

Strategies receive `known_at(data, decision)` physically filtered records: only completed available bars, effective available actions and FX observed and published by that time. The three provided strategies are stateless. The engine schedules buy-and-hold once and the other two on the first available daily cutoff in each month. Insufficient warm-up leaves cash until a later scheduled decision. Custom strategies without a `rebalance` property run daily.

The decision freezes quantity at known marks, FX and costs. The next scheduled open must be **strictly later** than the decision. The execution layer reads that open only; it does not read future high, low, close or volume for sizing. Prior completed volume is adjusted for intervening splits into current units. Missing expected opening data cancels the order rather than deferring it. A gap that breaks cash or risk limits cancels the frozen order rather than resizing it. A split between decision and execution cancels the pending order explicitly; quantity is not silently reinterpreted.

All venues execute in actual UTC order. Sells precede buys only within the same timestamp. Automatic sizing conservatively reserves only current cash and does not spend pending sale proceeds, including same-time projected sales. Consequently a monthly rebalance can hold cash until the next decision. This underinvestment is intentional and reported as a modeling limitation. The reducer can spend a completed sale's cash on a subsequent validated buy; a later venue's sale can never retroactively fund an earlier opening.

## Accounting, actions and valuation

EUR is the only cash balance. Quantities and the cash/FIFO ledger use `Decimal`; allowed precision is six decimal places, or explicit whole-share mode. Float conversion occurs for statistical reporting, after ledger reduction. No shorting or leverage is allowed. FIFO basis includes acquisition costs. Disposal matches allocate both basis and sale charges proportionally and preserve original acquisition timestamps. Metrics consume emitted `RealizedMatch` records, never reconstruct lots from price-adjusted fills.

For raw open \(P\), spread \(s\) and slippage \(l\), effective buy price is \(P(1+s/20000+l/10000)\); a sale uses the opposite signs. Commission is fixed EUR plus EUR consideration times commission basis points/10000. FX charge applies only to non-EUR consideration. A quote is **EUR per one unit of local currency**: multiply local consideration by the quote. No inverse-rate guessing occurs. Each cost enters cash once; report spread/slippage attribution does not subtract it again.

The newest FX observation known at the relevant timestamp must satisfy its configured elapsed-hour age. EUR uses an identity quote of one. Positive rates alone do not prove a source has supplied the correct direction; the imported FX source must honor the data contract.

Dividends create a total local-currency receivable at ex time, retained after selling shares. Equity includes the receivable until payment, so the raw ex-date price fall is not incorrectly counted as a loss without its entitlement. Payment uses FX known **at the exact payment time**, even when processing later. Dividend conversion fees are zero in this MVP. A split changes units and per-unit basis, preserving total basis.

Equity is EUR cash plus holdings at known raw marks and current known FX plus dividend receivables. Age-limited stale closes are valuation-only and flagged; split/dividend adjustments bridge an action that occurs before the next raw close is available. They are never executable prices. Exceeded age limits and unresolved delisting proceeds halt valuation. A missing expected bar produces a warning; the presence of a fresh cache does not turn an old observation into a current price.

The default illustrative settings are EUR 10,000 capital; EUR 1 fixed fee; 1 bp commission, 10 bp spread, 2 bp slippage and 5 bp foreign FX fee; 30% position cap, EUR 10,000 order cap, 1% prior-volume participation, 50 bp spread cap, and 96-hour price/FX ages. These are configurable educational inputs, not a broker tariff or personal recommendation. A one-asset run with the default 30% cap deliberately retains substantial cash.

## Statistical definitions

The equity frame columns are `timestamp` (aware UTC), `equity_eur`, `cash_eur`, `exposure`, `external_flow_eur`, `stale`. The initial row precedes transaction costs. Each supported union date contributes one daily row, not one row per venue or fill. Cash earns zero. Contributions are deferred: metrics explicitly reject nonzero external flows rather than label deposits as investment gains.

| Measure | Convention |
|---|---|
| Total return | Final equity / initial equity - 1 |
| CAGR and annualized return | Actual elapsed calendar time / 365.25; not arithmetic mean ×252 |
| Volatility | Sample daily standard deviation, `ddof=1`, ×√252 |
| Sharpe | Mean daily excess / sample excess standard deviation ×√252 |
| Risk-free daily rate | `(1 + annual_rate) ** (1/252) - 1` |
| Sortino | Mean excess / `sqrt(mean(min(excess,0)^2))` ×√252; all observations in downside mean |
| Maximum drawdown | Minimum of equity / cumulative peak - 1 |
| Duration | Maximum calendar time from peak through recovery or endpoint; unrecovered flag separate |
| Calmar | CAGR / absolute maximum drawdown |
| Exposure | Mean daily invested EUR / total EUR equity, excluding initial row |
| Turnover | Absolute traded EUR consideration / (2 × mean equity), including initial equity |
| Fill count | All executed fills |
| Win rate / profit factor | Completed FIFO matches only; gains / absolute losses for profit factor |
| Holding time | Arithmetic mean calendar holding days per completed FIFO match |
| Monthly / yearly returns | Compounded daily simple returns within the period |
| Worst periods | Minimum monthly/yearly compounded returns; period keys also exported |

Undefined metrics return `None` and a warning explaining insufficient observations or a zero denominator. No losing FIFO matches means undefined profit factor. An all-flat path has zero drawdown and undefined Sharpe/Sortino/Calmar. The 252 convention remains an approximation for a multi-venue union calendar. Extremely short-horizon CAGR can be unstable; it is never a forecast. Sample statistical reports use Pandas/NumPy, not a custom matrix/numerical engine.

The benchmark wrapper runs equal-weight initial buy-and-hold under exactly the same calendar, capital, limits, costs and next-open lag. Compare the two actual results. Benchmark-relative return can be computed as the difference in their `total_return` values; it is not claimed to be factor-adjusted alpha.

## Durable internal paper account

One repository root holds one EUR account. Construction persists initial capital; a different capital on restart rejects. `submit` begins an immediate SQLite transaction, checks canonical identity **before** present state/time validation, and returns the original fill for an identical replay. Identity includes order payload, dataset content hash, costs and limits. A changed payload under the same `PAPER-` ID rejects. New orders cannot move simulation time backward.

Actions, orders, fills, lots, cash, receivables and cursor commit atomically. Concurrent duplicate submissions produce one fill. A failure rolls back the entire transaction. Source action identities must remain consistent across datasets. `advance` processes historical action/payment events without requiring an artificial trade. A restart retains entitlements and the event cursor. No API key can add live execution.

`validate` is a no-write preview using a **complete selected historical dataset**, because it must locate the next scheduled open. At the lower level `validate_execution` explicitly receives an as-of `known: MarketData` context for currency and whole-portfolio concentration checks. The UI must not describe previewed historical execution as a live quote.

Journal entries append immutable revisions with a new entry ID; replaying an unchanged entry is idempotent. This preserves a reviewable history rather than silently overwriting lessons. Repository APIs expose transaction records; UI code does not need SQL.
